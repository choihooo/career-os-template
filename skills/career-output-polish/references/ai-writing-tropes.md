# AI Writing Tropes To Avoid

Source material adapted from the user-provided note based on `tropes.fyi`.

Use this file only during final output polishing. Do not load it during routine wiki maintenance unless the user specifically asks for style review.

## Goal

Detect and remove repeated AI writing patterns in resumes, portfolios, and related application materials. A single occurrence may be fine. The problem is clustering or habitual repetition.

## Word Choice

### Magic adverbs

Cut or replace repeated adverbs such as:

- quietly
- deeply
- fundamentally
- remarkably
- arguably

These often inflate ordinary claims without adding evidence.

### Overfamiliar AI vocabulary

Avoid habitual use of:

- delve
- certainly
- utilize
- leverage
- robust
- streamline
- harness

Use simpler words unless the exact term is necessary.

### Grandiose nouns

Be cautious with:

- tapestry
- landscape
- paradigm
- synergy
- ecosystem
- framework

Most resumes and portfolios are better with plain nouns.

### Pompous copula replacements

Reduce replacements for `is` or `are`, such as:

- serves as
- stands as
- marks
- represents

Use direct statements unless there is a real semantic difference.

## Sentence Patterns

### Negative parallelism

Avoid repeated structures like:

- `It's not X. It's Y.`
- `Not because X, but because Y.`
- `The question isn't X. The question is Y.`

These create artificial dramatic reframing.

### Countdown reveal

Avoid:

- `Not X. Not Y. Just Z.`

This often reads theatrical and thin.

### Self-posed rhetorical question

Avoid:

- `The result? ...`
- `The worst part? ...`

Do not manufacture suspense.

### Repeated sentence openings

Watch for several adjacent sentences beginning the same way, especially:

- `They... They... They...`
- `This... This... This...`

Vary rhythm naturally.

### Tricolon overuse

Do not stack lists of three repeatedly in adjacent lines or bullets. One is fine. Repetition is the tell.

### Empty transitions

Cut filler openers such as:

- It's worth noting
- Importantly
- Interestingly
- Notably

If the sentence matters, make it matter without announcing it.

### Shallow significance tails

Avoid sentence endings that claim importance without proving it, such as:

- highlighting its importance
- reflecting broader trends
- underscoring its role
- contributing to its legacy

## Paragraph And Tone

### Manufactured punch

Avoid too many short standalone fragments for emphasis.

### Listicle disguised as prose

Avoid serial paragraphing like:

- The first...
- The second...
- The third...

unless the structure truly needs numbering.

### Fake suspense

Cut phrases such as:

- Here's the kicker
- Here's the thing
- Here's where it gets interesting

### Patronizing analogy

Reduce:

- Think of it as...
- It's like...

Use analogy only when it genuinely clarifies.

### Futurist invitation

Avoid:

- Imagine a world where...

### False vulnerability

Cut polished fake self-awareness unless it is genuinely specific and necessary.

### "The truth is simple"

Do not assert clarity instead of demonstrating it.

### Stakes inflation

Avoid turning ordinary professional claims into world-historical claims.

### Hand-holding teacher voice

Reduce:

- Let's break this down
- Let's unpack this
- Let's dive in

### Vague attributions

Do not write:

- experts say
- observers note
- industry reports suggest

unless the sources are named.

### Invented concept labels

Avoid made-up analytical labels unless they are established terms in the field.

## Formatting

### Em-dash overuse

Use em dashes sparingly. If there are many on the page, replace most with commas, periods, or simple rewrites.

### Bold-first bullets

Avoid making every bullet start with a bold tag or label.

### Unicode decoration

Avoid decorative arrows or smart punctuation if plain text works.

## Composition

### Fractal summaries

Do not restate what the section will say, is saying, and just said.

### Dead metaphor repetition

If a metaphor appears more than once or twice, reassess it.

### Historical analogy stacking

Avoid rapid-fire company or technology analogies unless they materially advance the argument.

### One-point dilution

Cut repetition where the same claim is restated several ways.

### Content duplication

Check for repeated paragraphs, repeated claims, and same-point sections.

### Signposted conclusion

Avoid explicit closers like:

- In conclusion
- To sum up
- In summary

### "Despite its challenges"

Avoid the formula where a problem is mentioned only to be immediately neutralized by optimism.

## Resume-Specific Heuristics

- Prefer proof over atmosphere.
- Prefer direct claims over rhetorical framing.
- Prefer one strong line over three embellished lines.
- If a sentence sounds like a blog post, rewrite it.
- If two adjacent bullets have the same cadence, vary or compress them.

## Portfolio-Specific Heuristics

- Keep narrative grounded in decisions, tradeoffs, and outcomes.
- Cut performative insight unless it is backed by a real example.
- Avoid turning every section header into a slogan.
- Keep tone controlled even when positioning is ambitious.
